Tossana (ទស្សនៈ, "seeing") describes photos in Khmer: what is in the picture, who is doing what, and where. Small enough to run on a phone without internet (36 MB, about 0.2 s per photo on a CPU), so it can help people who cannot see the screen, and your photos never leave the device.

Why

Describing pictures in Khmer is hard for a machine

Today's captioning models were trained almost only on English. Khmer brings problems they never see.

No Khmer caption data

There is no large open set of photos with Khmer descriptions. Tossana starts from English captions translated to Khmer, with the test set checked by people.

No spaces between words

Khmer is written without spaces, so word-based scores do not fit. Tossana is scored on Khmer letter clusters, the same units Chakkhu and Sota use.

Seeing things that are not there

Small captioning models often name objects that are not in the photo. For someone who cannot see the picture, a wrong object is worse than a short caption.

Architecture · the plan

A small eye that speaks Khmer

About 33 million parameters: a pretrained ViT-S image encoder, a small decoder that writes Khmer clusters, a fast concept head and a guard against invented objects. It shares Sota's recipe: Khmer clusters as tokens, int8 ONNX, beam search.

Figure 1. Overview (planned design). 16×16 patches → 12 ViT blocks → a concept head (object tags) and a 4-layer caption decoder → beam search that drops any caption naming an object the concept head does not see.

Why this design

  • Starts from a trained eye. The ViT-S encoder already knows images (ImageNet); only the Khmer writer is learned from scratch.
  • Whole clusters, not letters. The same 3,402 Khmer cluster tokens as Chakkhu and Sota, so ស្ត្រី is one token and all three share one engine.
  • Checks itself. A small concept head lists the objects it sees; captions that name anything else are dropped (planned, the main research idea).
  • Runs on the phone. Encoder and decoder are exported to int8 ONNX (36 MB), like Sota: about 0.2 s per photo on a laptop CPU.

Benchmark

How good is it?

Scored on 1,910 test photos no model trained on: 1,000 everyday photos (Flickr8k, captions translated to Khmer) and 910 Cambodian scenes (phonsobon's Khmer captions). Data

Where the Khmer captions come from

There was no open Khmer captioning set big enough, so Tossana combines Khmer sets shared by others with English sets translated to Khmer. None of the Khmer sets states a license yet: research use, with credit.

Training (49,053 captions)

phonsobon v1 + v2Khmer descriptions of Cambodian scenes; duplicate photos removed across all splits2,157
Flickr8k → Khmereveryday photos, translated with MADLAD-400 (chosen over NLLB-200 by a 150-photo comparison)29,723
Angkor carvings → KhmerDarayut's heritage descriptions, translated and shortened769
News photo captionsrinabuoy; photo credits removed; training only, never scored16,331

Testing (never trained on)

Flickr8k test, in Khmer1,000 photos × 5 machine-translated captions1,000
phonsobon testCambodian scenes; a photo in any test split never reaches training910
Checked by people2 captions per photo read and fixed by a Khmer speakernext

Found while preparing: phonsobon v2 repeats some photos up to 11× across its own train and test splits, and v1 and v2 split the same photos differently. Tossana's rule: a photo belongs to its strictest split everywhere.

Roadmap

Where we are

One step at a time, each with a check before the next.

Done

Design & English check

The ≈33M design, then the same pipeline in English: CIDEr 65.9, int8 ONNX works.

Done

Khmer data

49k captions: phonsobon v1 + v2, Flickr8k and Angkor carvings translated with MADLAD-400, news.

Done

Train & publish

Exp 5 on one RTX 3090 (27 min). PyTorch, ONNX and demo are live.

Now

Checked test set

A Khmer speaker reads and fixes 2 captions for each test photo, so the scores become final.

Next

Guard against wrong objects

A concept head lists what it sees; captions that name anything else are dropped. The main research idea.

Later

In the apps

A browser demo like Sota's, and describing photos together with Chakkhu, offline.
